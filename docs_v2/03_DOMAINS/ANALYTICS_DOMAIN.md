# ATLS Platform: Analytics Domain Architecture

> **Confidential & Proprietary**  
> **Target Audience:** Principal Architects, Engineering Leads, AI Implementation Agents  
> **Domain:** Agricultural ERP & Operations Platform  

---

## 1. Domain Purpose
The Analytics Domain is the intelligence and insight layer of the ATLS platform. It is strictly a read-only projection engine. Its sole purpose is to consume, aggregate, and visualize data generated by transactional domains (Operations, Harvest, Inventory, Equipment, HR, Reporting) to provide actionable insights, forecasting, and historical benchmarks without ever mutating the underlying transactional truth.

## 2. Business Goals
*   **Operational Visibility:** Provide real-time dashboards for farm managers to monitor daily execution across all sectors.
*   **Performance Benchmarking:** Compare historical and seasonal yields, labor costs, and equipment utilization.
*   **Proactive Alerting:** Detect anomalies (e.g., sudden drop in yield, spike in fuel consumption) before they become catastrophic losses.
*   **Strategic Forecasting:** Use historical data to predict future resource needs and harvest yields to optimize supply chains.

## 3. Ubiquitous Language
*   **Projection:** A read-optimized, denormalized view of data derived from one or more domain events.
*   **KPI (Key Performance Indicator):** A quantifiable measure of performance over time for a specific objective.
*   **Dashboard:** A collection of visualizations (widgets) scoped to a specific role or operational area.
*   **Drill-down:** The UI interaction of clicking a high-level metric to view its constituent granular data points.
*   **Anomaly:** A data point that deviates significantly from the established historical trend or expected baseline.

## 4. Core Concepts
The core concept is **Separation of Read and Write (CQRS)**. The Analytics Domain never creates an Operation, hires a Worker, or logs a Breakdown. It solely listens for these events to construct fast, pre-calculated read models optimized for complex queries and charting.

## 5. Analytics Aggregate Philosophy
Unlike transactional domains, the Analytics Domain does not have traditional Aggregates designed to protect business invariants. Instead, it maintains **Read Models** and **Projections**. The closest equivalent to an aggregate is the `DashboardConfiguration` or an `AlertSubscription`, which manages how data is presented and monitored.

## 6. Read-Only Domain Rules
*   **CRITICAL RULE:** The Analytics Domain is 100% read-only regarding business data.
*   It cannot issue commands that change the state of Farm, HR, Operations, or Equipment entities.
*   If a user views an analytics dashboard and decides to change a schedule, that command is routed directly to the Operations Domain, bypassing Analytics entirely.

## 7. KPI Ownership Rules
*   Analytics centralizes the definition of KPIs to ensure consistency (e.g., preventing "Yield per Hectare" from being calculated three different ways in three different UI components).
*   KPI definitions are versioned. If the formula changes, historical projections may need to be rebuilt or annotated.

## 8. Projection Architecture
*   Projections are updated asynchronously via message queues or event streams (e.g., Kafka, Redis Streams) listening to core domain events.
*   Data is stored in denormalized structures (e.g., materialized views in PostgreSQL, or specialized OLAP databases/ElasticSearch).

## 9. Read Models
*   Read models are structured specifically for the UI components that consume them (e.g., `DailyHarvestSummaryReadModel`, `LaborCostPerEnclosureReadModel`).
*   They eliminate the need for complex, multi-table SQL JOINs during synchronous HTTP requests.

## 10. Aggregation Pipelines
*   Heavy calculations (e.g., calculating the average yield across 50 sectors for the past 5 years) are performed via background CRON jobs, Airflow pipelines, or stream processors.
*   Pipelines output pre-computed aggregates (hourly, daily, monthly summaries).

## 11. Event Consumption Strategy
*   The Domain subscribes to events like `OperationCompleted`, `HarvestYieldLogged`, `EquipmentBreakdownReported`.
*   Idempotency is critical: If an event is processed twice, the projection must not double-count the metric.

## 12. Operational KPIs
*   **Completion Rate:** Percentage of planned operations completed on time.
*   **Schedule Variance:** Average days operations are delayed.
*   **Input Efficiency:** Target vs. Actual chemicals/fertilizer applied per hectare.

## 13. Harvest KPIs
*   **Yield per Hectare:** Total output volume divided by active area.
*   **Quality Rejection Rate:** Percentage of harvest rejected at the packing facility.
*   **Harvest Pace:** Tons harvested per day vs. target timeline.

## 14. Inventory KPIs
*   **Stockout Frequency:** How often critical inputs run out during the season.
*   **Inventory Turnover:** The rate at which consumables are used and replenished.
*   **Wastage Rate:** Percentage of inventory expired or damaged.

## 15. Equipment KPIs
*   **Uptime/Availability:** Percentage of time machinery is available for field operations.
*   **Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF):** Average runtime between breakdowns.
*   **Fuel Efficiency:** Liters consumed per hour of operation or per hectare covered.

## 16. Labor KPIs
*   **Labor Cost per Hectare:** Total payroll cost attributed to a specific area.
*   **Productivity Rate:** Units (e.g., bins picked, trees pruned) completed per worker-hour.
*   **Overtime Ratio:** Percentage of total labor hours paid at an overtime rate.

## 17. Seasonal KPIs
*   **Cumulative Yield vs. Previous Season:** Year-over-year performance.
*   **Season-to-Date Costs:** Budget vs. Actual spend aggregated at the campaign level.
*   **Growing Degree Days (GDD) Impact:** Correlation between weather accumulation and harvest timing.

## 18. Completion Analytics
*   Tracking the lifecycle of workflows. E.g., "How many maintenance work orders are completed within 24 hours of creation?"

## 19. Cost Analytics
*   A unified view combining Labor Costs, Equipment Amortization/Fuel, and Inventory Inputs to determine the true Cost of Production per unit (e.g., Cost per Kg of Apples).

## 20. Yield Analytics
*   Geospatial analysis of yield across different enclosures, highlighting high-performing and under-performing soil/varieties.

## 21. Utilization Analytics
*   Are we over-staffed? Are we using our tractors to their full potential? Maps actual usage against theoretical maximum capacity.

## 22. Forecasting Architecture
*   Designed to plug into future Machine Learning models.
*   Current forecasting utilizes statistical moving averages and historical baselines to project future values (e.g., predicting next week's harvest volume based on this week's pace).

## 23. Trend Analysis
*   Identifying long-term directional shifts (e.g., "Labor productivity is trending downward over the last three months"). Requires smoothing algorithms to filter daily noise.

## 24. Benchmarking Rules
*   Allows comparing an Enclosure against the Farm average, or comparing current seasonal performance against the 5-year historical average.

## 25. Alert & Anomaly Engine
*   A rules engine that triggers when a KPI breaches a threshold (e.g., "Alert if Fuel Efficiency drops > 15% from the 30-day average").
*   Alerts are routed to the Notification Domain.

## 26. Dashboard Architecture
*   Dashboards are composition roots. They do not own data; they query various Read Models.
*   They support farm-level, sector-level, enclosure-level, seasonal-level, worker-level, and equipment-level scopes via context filters.

## 27. Dashboard Widget System
*   Widgets (Cards, Charts, Heatmaps) are standardized UI components.
*   Each widget is powered by a specific, dedicated API endpoint that returns perfectly formatted JSON, requiring zero client-side transformation.

## 28. Mobile Dashboard UX
*   Must support offline cached snapshots. A farm manager should be able to view yesterday's KPIs while completely offline in the field.
*   Touch-first interactions: Large tap targets, swipeable carousels for metrics, and simplified sparkline charts instead of dense, interactive data tables.

## 29. RTL Dashboard Rules
*   Charts (e.g., X-axis timelines) and data tables must correctly reverse flow for Arabic/Hebrew locales to maintain intuitive chronological reading.

## 30. CQRS Relationships
*   This domain is almost entirely **Queries**: `GetDailyDashboard`, `GetYieldTrend`, `GetLaborCostBreakdown`.
*   **Commands** are limited to configuration: `CreateDashboardLayout`, `SetAlertThreshold`.

## 31. Data Lake Future Architecture
*   To support BI integrations (Tableau, PowerBI), the Analytics Domain will eventually replicate its projections into a cloud Data Lake (e.g., Snowflake, BigQuery) for unconstrained ad-hoc querying.

## 32. Performance Constraints
*   Heavy calculations MUST NOT execute synchronously inside HTTP requests.
*   Dashboard API load times must be < 200ms. If a metric takes 5 seconds to calculate, it must be pre-calculated in a background pipeline.

## 33. AI Safety Rules
> [!CAUTION]
> **MANDATORY AI IMPLEMENTATION RULES**
> *   **No Transactional Mutation:** AI MUST NOT write any code in the Analytics Domain that alters data in the main transactional tables (e.g., `UPDATE operations_task`).
> *   **No Synchronous Aggregation:** AI MUST NOT write complex SQL `GROUP BY` aggregations over millions of rows inside a standard REST View. Use materialized views or background tasks.
> *   **Idempotent Event Handlers:** AI MUST ensure all event listeners handling projections can safely process the same event twice without corrupting the aggregate totals.

## 34. Anti-Patterns
*   **The Query of Death:** Running an un-indexed, multi-domain join query across 5 years of history directly from a UI request.
*   **Leaking Logic:** Putting complex mathematical formulas directly into the React frontend instead of the Backend Read Model.
*   **Stale Data Illusion:** Presenting 24-hour old cached data to a user without a clear "Last Updated" timestamp, leading to incorrect operational decisions.

## 35. Real-World Scenarios
**Scenario:** A Farm Manager checks their morning dashboard.
*   **Action:** Manager opens the mobile app.
*   **Read:** The app fetches the `DailyFarmSnapshotReadModel` (pre-calculated at 3:00 AM).
*   **Insight:** The dashboard shows an anomaly alert: "Spraying completion rate is 40% below target."
*   **Drill-down:** Manager taps the alert, viewing a chart comparing active machinery vs. planned machinery.
*   **Action Required:** The manager switches out of Analytics and into the Operations Domain to reassign a tractor.

## 36. Future Expansion Strategy
*   Integration with weather APIs to overlay meteorological data onto yield performance.
*   Integration with market pricing feeds to calculate real-time profitability projections.
*   Implementing advanced AI anomaly detection to flag issues before human-defined alert thresholds are breached.
